The quickest way to save a TikTok recipe is to tap the bookmark icon on the video, which adds it to your Favorites, ideally into a collection such as "Weeknight dinners". But a saved video isn't something you can cook from yet. To actually make it, you need the ingredients written down and the dish on a specific day of your week. Here's how to get from scrolling to cooking.

## Three ways to save a TikTok recipe

### 1. Favorites and collections inside TikTok

Tap the bookmark icon on the right side of the video. TikTok adds it to your Favorites, which you'll find on your profile behind the bookmark tab. From there you can create collections and sort videos into them, for example "Pasta", "Meal prep" or "Weekend projects".

This is the fastest option and keeps everything in one app. The downsides show up later:

- **It's still a video.** Amounts often flash on screen for a second or are only spoken, so you end up scrubbing back and forth with floury hands.
- **You can't search by ingredient.** "What can I make with the leek in my fridge?" is not a question your Favorites can answer.
- **It can disappear.** If the creator deletes the video or makes it private, it's gone from your Favorites too.

### 2. Screenshots and screen recordings

Pausing on the ingredient list and taking a screenshot is a popular workaround. It does survive the video being deleted, and it works offline. But screenshots end up buried between photos of receipts and parking spots, they lose the link to the creator, and a single screenshot rarely contains the full method.

### 3. Importing the link into a recipe app

Open the video, tap **Share**, then **Copy link** (or share straight to the app). A recipe app that understands social media links turns the video into a written recipe: a title, an ingredient list with amounts and the steps.

## Where the full recipe usually hides

Many recipe videos don't show everything on screen. Before you give up on one, check these places:

1. **The caption.** Tap "more" under the video; longer captions often contain the full ingredient list.
2. **On-screen text.** Pause where the ingredients appear. Some creators show them all at once at the start or end.
3. **The voiceover.** Amounts are often said aloud rather than written.
4. **"Recipe in bio".** The creator's profile links to a link-in-bio page or a blog with the written recipe. Visiting it is also the best way to support the creator.
5. **A pinned comment.** Creators sometimes pin the recipe or a link to it at the top of the comments.

If you'd rather not dig, a recipe extractor can do the searching for you.

## A 10-minute weekly routine that gets recipes cooked

Saving is easy. The step most people skip is deciding *when* to cook what they saved. This routine takes about ten minutes, once a week, ideally the day before you shop:

1. **Pick three or four saved recipes.** Not ten. Fill the rest of the week with meals you already know by heart, a leftovers night and maybe a takeout night.
2. **Get the written ingredient list for each one**, using the places above or by importing the link.
3. **Check what you already have** so you don't buy a third jar of paprika.
4. **Put each recipe on a specific day.** Match the effort to the day: the 15-minute noodle dish goes on Tuesday, the slow-cooked ragù on Sunday.
5. **Write one combined shopping list**, sorted by the stores you go to and the order you walk through them.
6. **When it's time to cook, keep the steps visible** without unlocking your phone every two minutes.

## Keep your saved recipes useful

A collection with 400 videos is as hard to use as no collection at all. Three habits keep it manageable:

- **Name collections by when you'd cook them**, not by cuisine: "20-minute weeknights", "Sunday cooking", "Make ahead", "For guests". When you plan your week, you know exactly where to look.
- **Prune every couple of months.** If you scroll past a saved recipe for the fifth time, unsave it. Your future self has spoken.
- **Keep the original link** with every recipe you write down or import, so you can rewatch a tricky step and credit the creator if you share it.

## FAQ

### How do I get the exact amounts from a TikTok recipe?

Check the caption first, then pause on any on-screen ingredient list, then look for a "recipe in bio" link or a pinned comment. If the amounts are only spoken, the [recipe extractor](/sapid/recipe-extractor) can listen to the video for you and write them down.

### Why did a recipe disappear from my TikTok Favorites?

Favorites only point to the original video. If the creator deletes it or makes their account private, it disappears from your Favorites as well. Write down or import the recipes you really care about.

### Can I save TikTok recipes without keeping the video?

Yes. Copy the video link and paste it into a recipe app or the free extractor. You'll get a written ingredient list, and with an app like Sapid, the full recipe with a link back to the video.
